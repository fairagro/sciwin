What Git does for versioning, SciWIn does for provenance.

Every command you run through `s4n` — or every connection you draw in SciWIn-Studio — is recorded: the artifacts involved, the exact command, and how they connect to the steps before and after them. That record is a graph, not just a log, and it can be re-executed, inspected, or handed to someone else, on their machine or on a remote server. See [Provenance & Publishing](provenance) for what that record actually contains and where it can go from there.

## Why not just a script?

A shell script or a notebook cell also "does the work," but it doesn't describe itself: nothing in `python analyze.py --input data.csv` says what `data.csv` actually is, what produced it, or what the script depends on. Re-running it correctly six months later, or on someone else's machine, means reconstructing all of that from memory, a README, or the shell history.

SciWIn's tool and workflow descriptions are the [Common Workflow Language](https://www.commonwl.org/) (CWL) instead — an open, YAML-based standard for describing command-line tools and how they connect, portable across plain Docker, HPC schedulers, and Kubernetes, and readable by any CWL-compliant runner, not only SciWIn's own. That's the deliberate trade against writing the same pipeline directly in Snakemake or Nextflow: both are capable tools, but a Snakefile only means something to Snakemake. A CWL `Workflow` document means the same thing to `cwltool`, to REANA, to `s4n execute`, or to a colleague using none of the above.

## What that buys you

- **A re-executable record, not just an audit trail.** How the provenance graph is built, annotated, and packaged for publishing is covered in [Provenance & Publishing](provenance).
- **A choice of execution backend without rewriting anything** — locally, in Docker, against a GA4GH TES server, or on a remote [REANA](https://reanahub.io) instance.
- **Two front ends over the same core** — SciWIn-Client on the terminal, SciWIn-Studio on a visual canvas, operating on the exact same on-disk project. See [Architecture](architecture) for how the pieces underneath both fit together.
